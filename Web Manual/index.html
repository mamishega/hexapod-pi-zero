1Introduction

1.1Purpose of this manual

This manual explains how to wire, set up, operate and maintain the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W version of the hexapod walking robot. It is written for the person building and driving the robot, and assumes basic familiarity with a computer terminal.

Read Section 2, Safety information before powering the robot for the first time.

1.2System overview

The hexapod is a six-legged walking robot with three servos per leg. A Pimoroni Servo 2040 board computes the walking gait and drives all 18 servos. In this version a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W is mounted on the robot. It receives an Xbox controller over Bluetooth, checks every movement, and sends walking commands to the Servo 2040 over a short serial (UART) link. No laptop is required to drive the robot.

1.3Feature availability

This version of the robot is under development. The table below shows which parts are available now and which are planned. Instructions for planned features are marked with a PLANNED notice.

2Safety information

2.1General safety

Warning Moving parts

The servos are strong enough to pinch fingers. Keep hands clear of the legs whenever the motors are enabled.

Warning Support the robot

Support the body with the feet off the ground the first time you power the robot, after any wiring change, and after any software update.

Caution Power off before wiring

Turn the main switch off before connecting or disconnecting any wire. Connecting a wire to a powered board can damage it.

Caution Start slowly

Begin with the slowest speed and a small step height. Increase them only after the robot walks steadily.

2.2Emergency stop methods

Learn all four ways to stop the robot before you drive it.

Note

If the Servo 2040 receives no command for 3 seconds (Pi crash, loose wire, flat Pi supply), it switches the motors off automatically. This is a software safeguard, not a hardware cut-off. Always keep the main switch within reach.

4Parts and tools

Tools: small screwdriver, wire strippers, multimeter (to check supply voltages and continuity), and a Mac or PC with Raspberry Pi Imager.

5Installation and wiring

5.1Before you begin

Verify Check the Servo 2040 pins

Confirm on the Servo 2040 pinout that its SDA pin is GP20 and its SCL pin is GP21. The serial link depends on this. If they differ, the board program must be changed to match before wiring.

Verify Check how the Servo 2040 is powered

Tested on this robot: the servo power terminal powers the servos only, not the Servo 2040’s processor. Without a 5 V supply on its USB-C port the board does not start, and the Pi reports “Board link: offline”. The lead from the Pi’s USB port (Section 5.4) provides this, so no laptop is needed.

5.2Wiring diagram

5.3Serial link connections

  • Both boards use 3.3 V logic. No level shifter is needed.
  • Keep the UART wires shorter than 20–30 cm and route them away from the servo power wires.

5.4Power connections

  • Battery + → main switch and fuse → Servo 2040 servo power terminal V+. The switch and fuse go on the + wire only.
  • Battery − → Servo 2040 V−, and → buck converter IN− (ground runs straight from the battery, not through the switch).
  • Main switch output (+) → buck converter IN+.
  • Buck converter OUT+ (5 V) and OUT− (GND) → Pi PWR IN port (the micro-USB port marked PWR IN, not USB). A micro-USB lead carries both wires.
  • Pi USB port (the inner micro-USB port, not PWR IN) → Servo 2040 USB-C. This powers the Servo 2040’s own processor. Use a micro-USB to USB-C lead, or an OTG adapter and a USB-C cable. When the LiDAR is fitted, a micro-USB OTG hub goes on this port and the Servo 2040 lead plugs into the hub.
  • All grounds are joined: battery −, buck converter, Pi and Servo 2040 GND.

Caution Separate Pi supply

Never power the Pi directly from the servo supply. Servo current spikes cause the Pi to restart while walking.

5.5RPLIDAR C1 laser scanner (planned)

Planned Planned

The wiring is shown on schematic Rev C. The driving service does not read the LiDAR yet.

  • Plug a micro-USB OTG hub into the Pi’s USB port. Plug the Servo 2040 USB-C lead into the hub.
  • Connect the RPLIDAR C1 to its USB adapter board (supplied in the kit) and plug the adapter into the hub. The adapter supplies 5 V and converts its 460 800 baud serial link to USB.
  • Why USB and not the GPIO pins: the Pi’s GPIO serial port (pins 8 and 10) is used by the Servo 2040, and its other serial port by Bluetooth for the controller.
  • Mount the scanner on top of the robot, level, with a clear view all round. Legs or wires in its view show up as obstacles.
  • Its signal pins use 3.3 V logic: never connect them to a 5 V signal.

Verify Check the LiDAR pinout

Before making your own lead, check the connector pinout on the Slamtec RPLIDAR C1 datasheet. With the kit’s adapter and lead, no check is needed.

6Raspberry Pi setup

6.1Prepare the microSD card

  1. Insert the card fully into the computer’s SD slot. On a microSD adapter, make sure the lock switch is not set to Lock.
  2. Open Raspberry Pi Imager.
  3. Select Device: Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W.
  4. Select Operating system: Raspberry Pi OS (other) → Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit).
  5. Select Storage: the 16 GB card. Check carefully: this erases the selected disk.
  6. Click Next, then Edit settings. On the General tab set hostname hexapod, username kangan and its password, the work Wi-Fi network Robocop and its password, Wi-Fi country AU, and your time zone.
  7. On the Services tab, turn on Enable SSH with password authentication.
  8. Click Save, then Yes. Writing and verifying takes 5–10 minutes.
  9. Re-insert the card if it was ejected. On the bootfs volume, open config.txt, add this line at the end, save, and eject the card:
    enable_uart=1

Note

enable_uart=1 fixes the Pi’s clock so the serial port on GPIO14/15 runs at a steady 115200 baud while Bluetooth keeps the main serial port for the controller. Do not disable Bluetooth. The software installer (Section 6.4) also turns off the Pi’s login console on this port, which would otherwise talk to the Servo 2040.

6.2First start-up and remote login

  1. Insert the card into the Pi and apply power. The first start-up takes 2–3 minutes.
  2. On the computer, open a terminal and log in as kangan. Type the password when asked (nothing is shown while you type):
    ssh kangan@hexapod.local
  3. If hexapod.local cannot be found, use the Pi’s IP address instead. Find it in the router’s list of connected devices (the Pi is named hexapod). For example:
    ssh kangan@10.52.126.57
  4. Update the system, then restart and log in again:
    sudo apt update && sudo apt full-upgrade -y
sudo reboot
  5. Install Node.js and check the version:
    sudo apt install -y nodejs npm
node --version

6.3Pair the Xbox controller

  1. On the Pi, start the Bluetooth tool and begin scanning:
    bluetoothctl
scan on
  2. Hold the Xbox button to switch the controller on, then hold the small pair button on its top edge until the logo flashes quickly.
  3. When Xbox Wireless Controller appears with its address, pair, trust and connect it (replace the address with the one shown):
    pair AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF
trust AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF
connect AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF
  4. Stop scanning and exit. The controller now reconnects automatically when switched on.
    scan off
exit

Note

Older Xbox One S controllers may not pair, or may report the wrong buttons. Install the xpadneo driver and pair again, or use a Series X|S controller.

6.4Install the hexapod software

The software is in the pi-service and Board Files folders next to this manual. The driving service starts automatically at boot once installed.

Note

Tested on the robot. Keep the body supported for the first test after any change.

  1. From the computer, copy the service to the Pi:
    scp -r pi-service kangan@hexapod.local:~/
  2. On the Pi, run the installer, then restart. It installs dependencies, turns the UART on and its login console off, and sets the service to start at boot:
    cd ~/pi-service
bash deploy/install.sh
sudo reboot
  3. With the main switch off, connect the Servo 2040 to the computer by USB-C. In Thonny, copy the four files from Board Files to the board, keeping calibration_offsets.json and joint_limits.json. Run check_and_restart.py, then close Thonny.
  4. Wire the serial link (Section 5.3) and power the robot with its body supported.
  5. On the Pi, stop the service and check the wiring. This never enables the motors:
    sudo systemctl stop hexapod-drive
npm run link-test
  6. Teach the service your controller’s buttons (once per controller model) and follow the prompts:
    npm run controller-setup
  7. Start the service:
    sudo systemctl start hexapod-drive
  8. Set up offline mode (the robot’s own Wi-Fi hotspot, Section 8.2). This does not change the current Wi-Fi connection:
    sudo bash deploy/wifi/install-wifi.sh

Caution Run the controller setup

Linux numbers the Xbox controller’s buttons differently depending on its driver. Without npm run controller-setup, buttons may do the wrong thing. Check with npm run controller-test before driving.

7Operation

7.1Pre-operation checklist

7.2Starting up

  1. Place the robot on its stand (feet off the ground) or in the clear area.
  2. Turn on the main switch. The Pi and the Servo 2040 start. The motors remain off.
  3. Wait 30–60 seconds for the Pi to start.
  4. Switch the Xbox controller on. The logo stops flashing when it is connected.
  5. Press A to enable the motors. The legs glide to the home stance.
  6. Press Y to stand, then push the left stick to walk.

Note

Steps 3–6 require the software installed in Section 6.4. To watch what the service is doing, run journalctl -u hexapod-drive -f on the Pi.

7.3Controller reference

7.4Adjusting walk settings

  • Hold a setting button to keep changing the value.
  • While walking, the robot blends into the new setting once you stop pressing.
  • A setting that would place a foot out of reach is refused, and the previous value is kept.
  • After B or View, move the stick again to walk. Holding it does not restart the walk.
  • If the controller switches off or its battery runs flat, the robot stops walking.

7.5Stopping and shutting down

  1. Release the sticks (or press B) and wait for the robot to stop.
  2. Press X to sit, then View to switch the motors off.
  3. Shut the Pi down from the computer so the microSD card is not damaged:
    ssh -t kangan@hexapod.local sudo shutdown -h now
  4. Wait until the Pi’s green light stops flashing (about 10 seconds), then turn off the main switch.

Caution

Turning off the main switch while the Pi is running can corrupt its microSD card.

Planned

A controller button combination to shut the Pi down safely without a computer.

8Phone and laptop access

8.1Live View (web page for teaching)

The Pi serves a Live View web page that shows, as it happens, how the robot moves and how a command travels from the controller to the servos. It is designed for showing students how the robot works. It starts automatically with the driving service; nothing needs to be installed on the viewing devices.

Note Quick start

Switch the robot on and wait about a minute. Join the Robocop Wi-Fi network, then open http://hexapod.local:5181 in a web browser. On a phone, use http://10.52.126.57:5181.

Launching the web app

There is nothing to start or install. The web app runs on the robot’s Raspberry Pi and starts by itself with the driving service each time the robot is switched on. Any device with a web browser can open it.

  1. Turn on the robot’s main switch and wait about 1 minute for the Raspberry Pi to start.
  2. Connect the phone, tablet or laptop to the same Wi-Fi network as the robot: at work this is Robocop. Away from a saved network, join the robot’s own network Hexapod-6607 instead (Section 8.2).
  3. Open a web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox) and type one of these addresses in the address bar:
    http://hexapod.local:5181
http://10.52.126.57:5181
http://10.42.0.1:5181
  4. Type http:// (not https://) and include :5181 at the end. If hexapod.local is not found, which is common on Android phones, use the number address instead.
  5. Bookmark the page, or on a phone or tablet choose Add to Home Screen, so it opens with one tap next time.
  6. To get a class connected, press Join at the top of the page and show it on the projector: students scan the QR code with their phone camera.
  7. Drive the robot with the Xbox controller as usual. The page updates on every viewer at the same time.

The number address (10.52.126.57) was correct when this manual was written. The router hands it out, so it can change after a restart. To find the current one, look for hexapod in the router’s list of devices, or log in to the Pi and run:

hostname -I

If the page does not open

  1. Check the device really is on the same network as the robot, and turn off mobile data on phones for a moment.
  2. Wait a little longer: the page is ready about 1 minute after power-on.
  3. If it works from one device but not from another on Robocop, the network may block devices from reaching each other. Use the robot’s hotspot instead (Section 8.2).
  4. Check that the driving service, which also runs the web app, is running. It should show active (running):
    systemctl status hexapod-drive
  5. If it is not running, restart it. First make the robot sit (X) and switch the motors off (View), because a restart drops the robot:
    sudo systemctl restart hexapod-drive

The page is split into five pages. On a laptop or larger screen they are listed in a side panel on the left; on a tablet or phone they are in a menu along the top. Each page has its own address (for example http://hexapod.local:5181/#servos), so one page can be projected or shared.

The Join button at the top shows how students open the page: the address with a QR code to scan and, on the robot’s own hotspot, the Wi-Fi name and password with a Wi-Fi QR code. The password is only shown to devices already on the hotspot. To put the join screen straight on a projector, open http://hexapod.local:5181/#join.

The sun/moon button at the top switches between light and dark themes (the page otherwise follows the device’s setting). The full-screen button is for projectors. To force a theme, add it to the address, for example http://hexapod.local:5181/?theme=light#live.

Note Watch only

Nothing on the page can move the robot: only the Xbox controller drives it. Up to 40 devices can watch at once. The pose shown is what the board is commanding; the servos have no position sensors, so a jammed or unpowered leg still appears to move.

Note Practise without the robot

On a Mac or PC with Node.js, open a terminal in the pi-service folder and run npm run live-demo, then open http://localhost:5182. A simulated robot and a scripted controller run a 75-second lesson (enable, walk, slow down, crab walk, turn, high steps, sit, stand) on repeat. Press Ctrl+C to stop.

Teaching tips: slow the walk down (D-pad ↓) so students can see which legs lift together; use the Top view to show the gait pattern and the Side view to show step height; ask students to predict which servo angle will change before pressing RB.

8.2Offline mode: the robot’s own Wi-Fi

Where there is no Wi-Fi network, or the robot is away from its saved network, the Pi runs its own Wi-Fi network (a hotspot). Laptops, tablets and phones join it directly, so the Live View and remote login still work with no router and no internet.

The Pi Zero 2 W does one or the other, not both. By default it chooses automatically (auto mode):

  • At power-on it looks for a saved network. If none is found, the hotspot starts about 75 seconds after power-on.
  • If the saved network is lost while running, the hotspot starts about a minute later.
  • While the hotspot runs with nobody connected, the Pi looks for a saved network again every 10 minutes. It never drops devices that are connected.
  • The robot itself keeps working throughout: the controller uses Bluetooth and the Servo 2040 uses the serial wires.
  1. On a laptop, tablet or phone, join the robot’s Wi-Fi network:
    Network:  Hexapod-6607
Password: hexapod2026
  2. If the device warns that the network has no internet, choose to stay connected.
  3. Open the Live View, or log in to the Pi:
    http://10.42.0.1:5181
ssh kangan@10.42.0.1

Caution Change the hotspot password

Everyone who knows the password can join the robot’s network and reach the Pi’s login prompt. Change it before using the robot with a class: on the Pi, edit /etc/hexapod/wifi.conf (sudo nano /etc/hexapod/wifi.conf), then run sudo bash ~/pi-service/deploy/wifi/install-wifi.sh to apply it. The password must be at least 8 characters.

Note

The hotspot uses 2.4 GHz channel 6, the only band the Pi Zero 2 W supports. With several robots in one room, each has its own network name. To add another saved network, use sudo nmtui on the Pi.

8.3Laptop on USB-C

Thonny (to copy board files) and Motion Studio (to train movements and calibrate legs) connect to the Servo 2040’s USB-C port. That port normally powers the board from the Pi (Section 5.4): with the main switch off, move the lead from the Pi to the laptop, and move it back afterwards.

  • Switch the motors off (View) before connecting a laptop, and do not drive with the controller while a laptop is connected.
  • Only one program can use the USB port at a time. Close Thonny before opening Motion Studio.
  • Keep the board’s calibration_offsets.json and joint_limits.json when copying new files.
