How a command travels

Message inspector

The real messages on the serial wires, newest first. Commands go from the Pi to the board; the board answers each one. Text is JSON, the same format web apps use.

    Bluetooth

    controller → Pi

    The controller sends the position of both sticks and every button by radio, many times a second. The Pi reads it 50 times a second and decides what the robot should do.

    UART serial wires

    Pi ↔ Servo 2040

    Two wires carry short text messages such as {"op":"walk"} at 115 200 bits per second. The board answers each one with its status: pose, servo angles and battery voltage.

    PWM pulses

    Servo 2040 → servos

    Each servo gets a pulse 50 times a second. The length of the pulse sets the angle to hold: a longer pulse turns it further (roughly 0.5 to 2.5 ms across its range). One signal wire per servo, 18 in all.

    The robot

    Inverse kinematics: the board knows where each foot should be and calculates the hip, upper-leg and lower-leg angles that put it there, for all six legs every 20 ms.

      Raspberry Pi

      Why it matters: a Pi that restarts or slows down stops sending commands, and after 3 seconds the Servo 2040 switches the motors off. Power dips are the usual cause: a weak 5 V converter or thin wires.

          How to recalibrate a servo
          1. Make the robot sit (X) and switch the motors off (View), then turn off the main switch.
          2. For a large offset: undo the horn screw, lift the horn off and put it back one tooth round (about 14°) in the direction that makes the offset smaller. Refit the screw.
          3. Move the Servo 2040 USB-C lead from the Pi to a laptop and open Motion Studio (user manual, Section 8.3).
          4. In the Leg calibration tab, choose the leg, adjust each joint until the leg matches the reference stance, and save.
          5. Move the lead back to the Pi, switch on, and check the leg here: its offset should now be small.
